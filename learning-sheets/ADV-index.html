How to use the sheets
  1. Learn mode: work through each learning sheet. Read the notes, step through the worked examples, then do the guided questions (each has a hint).

  4. The report scores you by objective. Below 80% on an objective links you back to its learning sheet.
Topic 1

Equivalent expressions

1.1

Polynomial operations and factoring

4 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Add, subtract and multiply polynomials, including the special products of binomials.
  2. Factor polynomials by taking out the greatest common factor and by factoring trinomials with a leading coefficient of 1 or other than 1.
  3. Recognize and factor differences of squares and perfect-square trinomials, and factor four-term polynomials by grouping.
  4. Determine unknown coefficients in polynomial identities and identify equivalent forms of an expression using structure.

1.2

Exponents and radicals

5 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Apply the laws of exponents, including zero and negative integer exponents, to simplify and evaluate expressions.
  2. Convert between radical form and rational exponent form (xm/n) and evaluate or simplify expressions with rational exponents.
  3. Simplify radicals by removing perfect-square factors, and add, subtract, multiply and divide radical expressions.
  4. Rewrite exponential expressions with a common base, such as 8x = 23x, to recognize equivalent forms.
  5. Solve for an unknown exponent or constant by writing both sides in the same form and matching exponents.

1.3

Rational expressions and rewriting formulas

5 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Simplify rational expressions by factoring, and determine the values excluded from their domain.
  2. Add, subtract, multiply and divide rational expressions.
  3. Divide a polynomial by a linear expression and use the remainder theorem to find remainders, factors and unknown coefficients.
  4. Rewrite a rational expression as a quotient plus a remainder over the divisor, and identify the constants in that form.
  5. Rearrange nonlinear formulas to isolate a specified variable.

Topic 2

Nonlinear equations in one variable and systems

2.1

Solving quadratic equations

5 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Solve quadratic equations by factoring and by taking square roots.
  2. Solve quadratic equations by completing the square and with the quadratic formula, including equations with irrational solutions.
  3. Rearrange an equation into a solvable form and choose the most efficient solution method.
  4. Determine the sum or the difference of the solutions of a quadratic equation directly, without necessarily finding each solution.
  5. Solve quadratic equations that model real situations, such as projectiles and areas, and interpret the solutions, rejecting any that do not fit the context.

2.2

Discriminant, number of solutions and roots

5 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Use the discriminant b2 − 4ac to determine the number of real solutions of a quadratic equation, and connect it to the x-intercepts of a parabola.
  2. Determine the values of a constant that give a quadratic equation exactly one, two or no real solutions.
  3. Find the sum and product of the roots of a quadratic equation using {−b/a} and {c/a}, without solving it.
  4. Build a quadratic equation or function from its roots, or from a given sum and product of roots.
  5. Solve and analyze quadratic equations that contain parameters, such as finding a constant from a known root or writing the solutions in terms of a constant.

2.3

Radical, rational and absolute value equations

4 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Solve radical equations by isolating the radical and squaring, and reject extraneous solutions by checking in the original equation.
  2. Solve rational equations by clearing denominators, identifying excluded values and rejecting extraneous solutions.
  3. Solve absolute value equations of the form |ax + b| = c and |expression| = |expression|, and determine how many solutions they have.
  4. Solve equations with rational exponents, such as x3/2 = 27, by rewriting powers as roots and raising both sides to the reciprocal power.

2.4

Systems of linear and nonlinear equations

5 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Solve a system of a linear equation and a quadratic equation by substitution, and use the solutions to answer what is asked.
  2. Determine the number of solutions of a linear–quadratic system by using the discriminant, including when the line is tangent to the parabola.
  3. Solve systems that involve a circle or an absolute value equation together with a linear equation.
  4. Interpret the solutions of a system as intersection points and read or verify them from a graph or table.
  5. Find the value of a constant that gives a nonlinear system exactly one, two or no real solutions.

Topic 3

Nonlinear functions

3.1

Quadratic functions and parabolas

5 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Identify the y-intercept, the vertex and the x-intercepts of a parabola from its standard, vertex or factored form.
  2. Determine the axis of symmetry and the vertex using x = −{b/2a} or symmetry, and find the maximum or minimum value of a quadratic function.
  3. Convert a quadratic between standard, vertex and factored forms, including by completing the square.
  4. Match quadratic equations with their graphs using the sign of a, the intercepts and the vertex.
  5. Interpret and solve quadratic models in context, such as maximum height, landing time and maximum revenue or profit.
Open sheet 3.1
3.2

Exponential functions and models

5 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Identify the initial value and the growth or decay factor of f(x) = a · bx from an equation, a table or a graph, and use them to evaluate the function.
  2. Write and use exponential models from a percent change (b = 1 + r or b = 1 − r), a doubling time or a half-life.
  3. Convert exponential models and rates between time units, using forms such as a · bt/k.
  4. Distinguish linear from exponential relationships in tables, graphs and verbal descriptions.
  5. Interpret the constants and expressions in an exponential model in the context of the situation.
Open sheet 3.2
3.3

Polynomial functions, zeros and graphs

5 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Connect the zeros, factors and x-intercepts of a polynomial function by using the factor theorem.
  2. Determine the remainder when a polynomial is divided by x − a, and use remainders to find unknown values.
  3. Interpret the graph of a polynomial function: its zeros, where it is positive or negative, the effect of multiplicity, and its end behavior.
  4. Analyze absolute value functions through their equations, graphs and real-world meaning.
  5. Determine the domain and range of nonlinear functions from equations, graphs and contexts.
Open sheet 3.3
3.4

Transformations, composition and nonlinear models

5 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Determine how the translations f(x) + k and f(x − h) move a nonlinear graph, its points, its vertex and its intercepts.
  2. Determine the effect of the reflections −f(x) and f(−x) and the vertical stretch a·f(x), alone or combined with shifts, on a graph and its equation.
  3. Evaluate, write and interpret composite functions f(g(x)) in which one or both functions are nonlinear.
  4. Evaluate functions, transformed functions and composites from tables and graphs.
  5. Choose an appropriate nonlinear model for a context or a table and interpret its constants and values.
