How to use the sheets
  1. Learn mode: work through each learning sheet. Read the notes, step through the worked examples, then do the guided questions (each has a hint).
  2. Tick “I can do this objective” when you are confident, and read the Recap of common traps.
  3. Quiz Sheet 1 · Practice (easy to hard), then Quiz Sheet 2 · SAT Level (Module 2 standard, ending with Challenge questions).
  4. The report scores you by objective. Below 80% on an objective links you back to its learning sheet.
Topic 1

Linear equations in one variable

1.1

Solving linear equations

4 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Solve multi-step linear equations by distributing, combining like terms and using inverse operations.
  2. Solve linear equations that have the variable on both sides.
  3. Solve linear equations containing fractions or decimals by clearing them or working with them directly.
  4. Determine the value of an expression or an unknown constant from a linear equation by using its structure.

Number of solutions and literal equations

4 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Determine whether a linear equation in one variable has exactly one solution, no solution or infinitely many solutions.
  2. Determine the value of a constant (or constants) that gives a linear equation no solution, infinitely many solutions or a specified solution.
  3. Solve a literal equation or formula for a specified variable, including when that variable appears in more than one term.
  4. Apply rearranged formulas in real-world contexts to express or calculate one quantity in terms of the others.

Writing linear equations from words

Learning objectives

Slope, intercepts and forms of a line

4 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Calculate the slope of a line from two points, including points with unknown coordinates or real-world rise and run
  2. Identify the slope, x-intercept and y-intercept of a line from an equation in any form
  3. Convert a linear equation between slope-intercept, standard and point-slope forms
  4. Write the equation of a line from two points, a point and a slope, or its intercepts

  1. Determine the slopes of parallel and perpendicular lines, including lines in standard form, and write their equations.
  2. Identify and write equations of horizontal and vertical lines and use them to locate points.
  3. Determine whether a point lies on a line and find a missing coordinate or constant.
  4. Write the equation of a line from its graph or from graph features such as intercepts and plotted points.

Topic 3

Linear functions

3.1

Function notation and evaluating

5 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Evaluate a function at a number or an expression, including forms such as f(2a), f(a + 1) and f(a) + f(b).
  2. Solve equations of the form f(x) = k for the input, including in real-world contexts.
  3. Determine a linear function, and the values it takes, from a table of values or a pair of points.
  4. Evaluate and write compositions of linear functions such as f(g(x)).
  5. Interpret and write transformations of linear functions such as g(x) = f(x − h) + k.

3.2

Rate of change and writing linear functions

5 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Determine the slope (rate of change) of a line from two points, a table, or a graph.
  2. Write a linear function in the form f(x) = mx + b from two points, two function values, or a table.
  3. Identify whether a linear function is increasing, decreasing, or constant from its slope, table, or graph.
  4. Determine a rate of change in a real-world situation and state it with the correct units.
  5. Determine whether a table of values represents a linear function by comparing rates of change.

3.3

Interpreting linear models

5 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Interpret the slope, intercepts and other constants of a linear model in context, including models written in non-standard forms.
  2. Use a linear model, or build one from two data points, to make predictions in both directions (input to output and output to input).
  3. Compare two linear models and determine when they are equal and when one overtakes the other.
  4. Rewrite a linear model after converting the units of its input, its output or its rate.
  5. Determine a reasonable domain for a linear model and identify conclusions that go beyond its limits.

Topic 4

Systems of two linear equations

4.1

Solving systems

5 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Solve a system of two linear equations by substitution, including setting two expressions for y equal.
  2. Solve a system of two linear equations by elimination, scaling one or both equations when needed.
  3. Interpret the solution of a system as the intersection point of two lines and find it algebraically or with Desmos.
  4. Determine the value of an expression such as x + y or 2x − y directly by combining the equations.
  5. Solve systems whose equations contain fractions or decimals by clearing them first.

4.2

Number of solutions in systems

4 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Determine whether a system of two linear equations has exactly one solution, no solution or infinitely many solutions by comparing slopes, intercepts or coefficients.
  2. Find the value of a constant that makes a system have no solution, infinitely many solutions or exactly one solution.
  3. Interpret the number of solutions from graphs (intersecting, parallel or coincident lines) and from the result of solving algebraically.
  4. Choose or write a second equation that gives a system a required number of solutions.

Topic 5

Linear inequalities

5.1

Solving one-variable and compound inequalities

4 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Solve one-variable linear inequalities, reversing the inequality sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative number, including inequalities with fractions or a constant parameter.
  2. Solve compound “and” and “or” inequalities and describe the resulting solution set.
  3. Solve absolute-value inequalities of the form |x − a| < b or |x − a| > b, and write an interval as an absolute-value inequality.
  4. Determine integer solutions of an inequality and identify which given values satisfy it.
Open sheet 5.1
5.2

Two-variable inequalities and their graphs

4 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Determine whether an ordered pair is a solution to a two-variable linear inequality or to a system of inequalities.
  2. Identify the graph of a linear inequality or system: solid or dashed boundary, the shaded side, and the inequality that matches a given graph.
  3. Write and interpret linear inequalities and systems that model real-world limits such as budgets, capacities and goals.
  4. Determine the values of a constant or parameter that place a point inside (or outside) a solution region, or that give a system solutions.

5.3

Inequality word problems

4 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Write an inequality that models a situation, translating phrases such as “at least,” “at most,” “more than” and “no more than” into the correct symbol.
  2. Solve inequalities that model budgets and constraints, and interpret the solution set in context.
  3. Determine the maximum or minimum whole-number value that satisfies a constraint, rounding in the correct direction.
  4. Write and interpret systems of inequalities that model several constraints at once, and test whether a combination satisfies all of them.
