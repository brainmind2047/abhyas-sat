Topic 1

Lines, angles and triangles

1.1

Lines, angles and polygons

5 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Find unknown angle measures, including angles written as expressions, using vertical angles, linear pairs, and complementary and supplementary angles.
  2. Identify corresponding, alternate interior and same-side interior angles formed when parallel lines are cut by a transversal, and use them to find angle measures.
  3. Write and solve equations for angles given as algebraic expressions in figures with parallel lines and a transversal.
  4. Apply the interior angle sum (n − 2) · 180° to find a missing angle, an angle sum or the number of sides of a polygon.
  5. Determine the interior and exterior angles of regular polygons and use the fact that the exterior angles of a convex polygon add up to 360°.

1.2

Triangle properties and congruence

5 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Apply the triangle angle sum of 180° to find missing angles, including angles written as algebraic expressions.
  2. Use the exterior angle theorem to find exterior angles, remote interior angles and unknown values.
  3. Use the equal sides and equal angles of isosceles and equilateral triangles to find unknown angles and lengths.
  4. Determine possible side lengths with the triangle inequality, and order sides and angles using the fact that the largest side is opposite the largest angle.
  5. Identify congruent triangles by SSS, SAS, ASA or AAS, and use corresponding parts to find lengths and angles.

1.3

Similar triangles

5 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Identify similar triangles by AA, including triangles formed by a line parallel to a side and overlapping or nested triangles, and write a similarity statement with the vertices in matching order.
  2. Determine the scale factor between similar triangles and use proportional corresponding sides to find missing lengths, including sides given as algebraic expressions.
  3. Find missing lengths in nested and crossed (hourglass) figures formed by segments parallel to a side of a triangle.
  4. Solve problems with similar right triangles, including shadow, lamppost and sight-line problems and the altitude drawn to the hypotenuse.
  5. Apply the fact that the ratio of the perimeters of similar triangles equals the scale factor.

Topic 2

Area and volume

2.1

Perimeter and area of plane figures

5 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Calculate the perimeter and area of rectangles, triangles, parallelograms and trapezoids, using a height that is perpendicular to the base.
  2. Calculate the circumference and area of a circle or semicircle, and find the radius from a given circumference or area.
  3. Determine the area or perimeter of composite and shaded-region figures by adding and subtracting the areas of simpler shapes.
  4. Determine a missing dimension from a given area or perimeter, including figures whose side lengths are algebraic expressions.
  5. Determine the area and perimeter of figures in the xy-plane from the coordinates of their vertices.

2.2

Volume and surface area of solids

5 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Calculate the volume of rectangular prisms, other right prisms and cylinders by multiplying the area of the base by the height.
  2. Calculate the volume of cones, pyramids, spheres and hemispheres by using the formulas on the DSAT reference sheet.
  3. Determine the total or partial surface area of rectangular prisms, triangular prisms and cylinders.
  4. Determine a missing dimension of a solid, such as a radius, height or edge length, from its volume or surface area.
  5. Solve composite-solid and fill or capacity problems, including conversions between cubic units and liters.

2.3

Scale factors and changing dimensions

5 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Determine how the perimeter and area of a plane figure change when every length is multiplied by a scale factor k.
  2. Determine how the surface area and volume of a solid change when every length is multiplied by a scale factor k.
  3. Calculate the new area or volume when only one dimension changes, or when different dimensions change by different factors.
  4. Find the scale factor and missing measures of similar figures and solids from a ratio of lengths, areas or volumes.
  5. Calculate the percent change in area or volume when dimensions change by a given percent.

Topic 3

Right triangles, trigonometry and circles

3.1

Pythagorean theorem and special right triangles

5 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Apply the Pythagorean theorem to find a missing side of a right triangle, and use its converse to determine whether a triangle is a right triangle.
  2. Identify Pythagorean triples and their multiples and use them to find missing side lengths quickly.
  3. Determine all side lengths of a 45°-45°-90° or 30°-60°-90° triangle from any one side.
  4. Calculate the distance between two points in the xy-plane.
  5. Determine the diagonals of rectangles and rectangular boxes and the altitudes of equilateral and isosceles triangles.
Open sheet 3.1
3.2

Right-triangle trigonometry

5 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Identify the opposite side, adjacent side and hypotenuse for an acute angle of a right triangle, and write its sine, cosine and tangent as ratios of side lengths.
  2. Determine a missing side length of a right triangle from one side and either an acute angle or a given trigonometric ratio.
  3. Use the complementary-angle relationship sin x° = cos(90° − x°) to relate the ratios of the two acute angles and to solve for unknown angles or values.
  4. Explain why trigonometric ratios are equal in similar right triangles, and use one given ratio to find the other ratios with a Pythagorean triple or the Pythagorean theorem.
  5. Solve real-world problems involving angles of elevation and depression, ladders, ramps and other right-triangle models.

3.3

Circles: arcs, angles and sectors

5 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Calculate the radius, diameter, circumference or area of a circle from any one of these measurements.
  2. Determine arc measures, arc lengths and sector areas from a central angle in degrees by treating each as a fraction of the whole circle.
  3. Convert between degrees and radians, and use radian measure to find arc length, sector area or a central angle.
  4. Apply the relationship between inscribed and central angles, including angles inscribed in a semicircle, to find angle and arc measures.
  5. Solve for lengths in circles by using the facts that a tangent is perpendicular to a radius and that the perpendicular from the center bisects a chord.

3.4

Circles in the coordinate plane

5 learning sheets · 12 guided questions · 2 quiz sheets × 20

Learning objectives
  1. Identify the center and the radius of a circle from its equation in standard form.
  2. Write the equation of a circle from its center and radius, from its center and a point on it, or from its graph.
  3. Determine the equation, center or radius of a circle from the endpoints of a diameter by using the midpoint and distance formulas.
  4. Rewrite a circle equation from general form into standard form by completing the square to find its center and radius.
  5. Determine whether a point lies inside, on or outside a circle by comparing its squared distance from the center with the square of the radius.
